# The Keluri — speakers of Kelunta

Setting: Planet #06cy8vf7zvurfgpbrgnm4l, continent **Meridia**, the northwestern
freshwater lakeland.

Labels follow the Orogen convention: `MEASURED` (generator data), `DERIVED`
(physical model), `INTERPRETED` (inference consistent with the data),
`INVENTED` (creative addition, mine).

---

## 1. The land

`MEASURED` Meridia is the planet's highest continent — mean elevation 2.15 km,
peak 7.54 km at the Central Massif, mean temperature 14.2 °C. Its climate splits
A 24% / **B 30%** / C 20% / D 8% / E 18%.

`MEASURED` The continent's structure runs NW–SE: the Western Collision Front and
Central Massif form a mountain backbone; east of that wall lies the Arid Interior
Plateau, dry and leeward and dominant in area. The far north is subarctic Cold
Highlands.

`DERIVED` In the continent's northwest sits a cluster of **freshwater lakes** —
described in the planetary record as "a structural gift in an otherwise dry
plateau interior," and flagged as a contested water source.

`INTERPRETED` That combination fixes the local conditions. The lakes sit behind
the Collision Front, so they are in its rain shadow: the surrounding plateau is
Köppen B, cold-arid (BSk) rather than hot-arid, because of altitude and northern
latitude. Elevation around the basin runs high — well above the continental mean,
plausibly 2.5–3 km. Winters are long and hard; summers are short, bright, and dry.
Precipitation is low, but the lakes are fed by snowmelt off the Front, not by
local rain. The lakes are therefore **reliable in a landscape that is not**.

`INVENTED` The basin is called **Marun** — the low ground. The lakes themselves
are *kelu*: standing water, water that does not leave. The distinction matters to
the Keluri and it will matter to the grammar.

## 2. Subsistence

`INTERPRETED` A cold, high, arid basin with permanent fresh water supports a
narrow but stable economy:

- **Lake-margin cultivation** on the strip of land that snowmelt reaches. Frost-
  tolerant tuber and grain crops, short growing season, intensive rather than
  extensive.
- **Herding** on the surrounding dry plateau — animals that can walk between
  water sources and convert sparse forage. Transhumance: up to high pasture in
  the short summer, down to the lake margin for winter.
- **Lake fishing and reed harvesting.** Reeds for boats, roofing, cordage,
  writing surfaces.

`INTERPRETED` Nothing here supports a large population. The Keluri are numerous
enough to have towns at the lake mouths and thin enough that most of the plateau
is empty. Their wealth is not land — it is **access**.

---

## 3. Society

`INVENTED` Because the lakes are the only reliable water for a thousand
kilometres in three directions, Keluri society is organised around **allocation
rather than ownership**. The governing institution is not a chief but a reckoning:
each year, representatives of the lake-margin settlements meet to re-cut the
irrigation shares against the previous winter's snowpack. Water is measured,
witnessed, and remembered.

`INVENTED` Consequences that will feed directly into the language:

- **Obsessive evidential precision.** A community that settles water rights by
  testimony develops habits about the *source* of a claim. Did you see the
  snowpack, or were you told about it? Kelunta is expected to mark this.
- **A rich vocabulary of quantity, share, and division** — and of debt, in the
  sense of a share owed forward to next year.
- **Verbs of staying versus leaving.** *Kelu* (standing water) against water that
  runs off and is lost is the culture's founding contrast. Expect it to
  grammaticalise.

## 4. Neighbours and contact

`INTERPRETED` Four directions, four different relationships:

| Direction | Who / what | Relationship |
|---|---|---|
| **West** | The Western Collision Front and its pass communities; beyond it, the wet windward coast | Trade through the passes — the Keluri are the first stop east of the wall. Highland pidgin contact. |
| **East** | Arid Interior Plateau: salt pans, oasis corridors, caravans | Salt-for-grain exchange. Caravans need water; the Keluri sell it. This is the main axis of outside contact. |
| **North** | Cold Highlands, subarctic frontier | Sparse, seasonal, mostly hunters. Little contact, some raiding. |
| **South** | The plateau falls away toward the SW Trunk River and its delta port | Long, indirect. The delta is where Keluri goods eventually reach the sea and where distant news arrives from. |

`INTERPRETED` This is a **hinge position**: not isolated, but not on anyone's
highway either. Everyone passing between the wet west coast and the salt interior
comes through, stays a few days, and leaves. That is exactly the contact profile
that produces a language with a stable core and a layer of trade loanwords — and
one whose speakers are used to being multilingual without being assimilated.

---

## 5. Why this matters for Kelunta

The design consequences I am carrying into Lesson 2 and beyond:

1. **Evidentiality is not decoration** — it is what the water reckoning runs on.
   It should be obligatory and grammatical, not a set of adverbs.
2. **Standing vs. leaving** is the culture's core semantic contrast and a
   candidate for aspect or verb classification.
3. **A trade-contact layer** in the lexicon (salt, pack animals, measures, coastal
   goods) that is phonologically identifiable as foreign — a lever for Lessons 7,
   10 and 12.
4. **A clean, moderate phonology** fits a language whose speakers use it across
   a linguistic frontier as a second language for outsiders — but that is an
   aesthetic choice, made in Lesson 1, not a claim that geography determines
   sound.
