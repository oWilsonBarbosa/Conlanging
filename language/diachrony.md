# Diachrony — Proto-Alantian → Alantian

> Alantian is built with an internal history: older ("Proto-Alantian", PA) forms plus
> **regular sound changes**. Much of the modern morphophonology — gemination, voiced
> stops, the ban on obstruent codas, the tense alternations — is the **residue** of these
> changes, not arbitrary synchronic rules.

## Prosody of the proto-stage (working assumption)

PA had **fixed initial stress** (e.g. *tápa-), which conditioned syncope. This feeds the
deferred suprasegmental decision (L10/11) — modern stress likely descends from it.

## Ordered sound changes

| # | Change | Statement |
|---|---|---|
| **S1** | Post-tonic syncope | An unstressed short vowel in an open, non-final syllable deletes: *CV́CV-CV → CV́C-CV. |
| **S2** | Post-nasal voicing | A voiceless stop **voices after a nasal** coda: *m‑t > m‑d, *n‑k > n‑g. |
| **S3** | Nasal place assimilation | A nasal coda takes the **place of the following stop**: m → n /_d, n → ŋ /_g, etc. |
| **S4a** | Gemination | A coda stop + an **identical** onset → a geminate (p‑p → pp). |
| **S4b** | Obstruent vocalization | A remaining **coda obstruent** becomes a glide: **labial → u**, **non-labial (coronal/velar) → i**, forming a diphthong with the preceding vowel. |

**Codas that survive untouched:** sonorant liquids /l r/, nasals (→ homorganic via S2–S3),
and /s/. After these changes the only possible codas are **sonorant, /s/, or a
geminate-half** — exactly the modern CV(R) constraint.

## Tense by reduplication

Tense = **reduplicate one edge** of the stem; the doubled syllable then undergoes S1, and
the resulting cluster resolves by S2–S4.

- **Non-past = final(rightward)-syllable reduplication** → a **medial geminate**.
- **Past = initial(leftward)-syllable reduplication** → an **initial cluster**
  (→ diphthong / voiced-nasal cluster / geminate / legal coda).

### Worked derivations

| stem (C₁aC₂a) | PST — *initial* redup | N.PST — *final* redup |
|---|---|---|
| *tapa* (t…p) | *ta.tapa > *tatpa > **taipa** (t→i) | *tapa.pa > **tappa** |
| *mata* (m…t) | *ma.mata > *mamta > *mamda > **manda** (voicing + assim.) | *matata > **matta** |
| *naka* (n…k) | *na.naka > *nanka > *nanga > **nanga** [naŋga] | *naka.ka > **nakka** |
| *sapa* (s…p) | *sa.sapa > *saspa > **saspa** (s = legal coda) | *sapa.pa > **sappa** |
| *kasa* (k…s) | *ka.kasa > *kaksa > **kaisa** (k→i) | *kasa.sa > **kassa** |

So **non-past always shows a medial geminate**, while **past shows the root's first
consonant as a coda** — surfacing as a diphthong (obstruent → glide), a prenasalized
voiced cluster (nasal), a geminate (identical), or a plain *s*-cluster.

## What the modern grammar inherits (residue)

- **Geminates** ← S4a fused C+C.
- **Voiced stops /b d g/ and the [ŋ] allophone** ← S2–S3 in post-nasal clusters.
- **Obstruents are onset-only** ← S4 vocalized/geminated away every obstruent coda (→ CV(R)).
- **Tense alternations** (tappa ~ taipa, matta ~ manda) ← reduplication + S1–S4.
