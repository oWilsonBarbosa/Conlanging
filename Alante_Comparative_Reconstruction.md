## 0. Status and Methodological Standing — READ FIRST

This document is **design-mode (creative), not evidence-mode (analytical).** It must be read alongside, and is subordinate to, three derived-only documents that govern the evidence layer of this project:

- `Alante_Cognate_Check.md`
- `Alante_Inventory_Consolidation.md`
- `Alante_Synchronic_Areal_Profile.md`

**The verdict of the evidence layer is that the Alante lexicons are NOT cognate.** Across the 53 meanings shared by Angélico and Pavanam, mean form-similarity is ~0.21 (chance level), only one pair ("water," eʋes ~ eihes) exceeds 0.5, and **no recurring sound correspondences exist** — the actual signature of genetic descent. The word-forms were invented independently, not derived from a common ancestor by sound change.

That verdict is correct on the data, and it directly constrains how this document may be used:

1. **The "sound laws" here are stipulations, not findings.** Each of the new laws proposed in §2.2 (C21 `*w→b`, C22 `*bʱ→m`/coda, C23 `*ŋr` metathesis) and §3 (V2, V3) rests on a **single** cognate set. A genuine sound law requires the same correspondence recurring across many words; a correspondence seen once is curve-fitting, not evidence. Treat them as *design proposals* — "if the family were built cognate, this is how the forms could relate."

2. **Only Tier 1 has even the start of empirical support,** and only weakly: `*w → ʋ` in Angélico recurs twice (eʋes "water", madiua "grass"), but the same two words send Pavanam's `*w` to two different outcomes, so even the anchor is inconsistent. Everything at Tier 2/3 is below the noise floor by comparative-method standards.

3. **This corresponds to the Cognate Check's path (C) — "generate cognates (creative, not analytical)."** The document answers a design question (*if* a genetically coherent family is the goal, here is a reconstruction to build toward), not a descriptive one. Its forms become real evidence only if the daughter lexicons are actually rebuilt *from* ProtoAlante by these sound changes — construction work that has not been done.

4. **Source-selection differs from the derived-only layer.** This document uses the declared inventories, `ProtoAlante_OLD.xlsx`, and Classical Alantian; the derived-only documents exclude all three and admit only lexicon-attested segments. Where inventory counts disagree (e.g. Angélico 17C here vs. 15C derived-only), the derived-only figure is the evidentiary one; the difference is admissibility rules, not a factual dispute.

In short: **keep this for its design value; do not cite it as proof of descent.** The genealogy it describes is declared design intent, not a result recovered from the lexicon.

## 1. Premises and Scope

The Comparative Method operates on three requirements: (1) two or more attested daughter forms sharing the same meaning, (2) systematic phonological correspondence (not accidental similarity), and (3) the impossibility of borrowing as a simpler explanation. Applying this to the Alante family presents a structural problem: most Angélico and Pavanam vocabulary for the same concept appears to come from *different roots entirely*. This is itself a philological finding and is treated as such in §6 (The Lexical Replacement Problem).

Where cognates are identified, they are assigned to one of three tiers:

| Tier | Criterion | Symbol | Evidentiary weight |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 — Definite | Forms identical or differ only by a correspondence seen ≥2× | ✦ | Weak but real |
| 2 — Probable | Forms derivable through a correspondence proposed from a single example | ◆ | Stipulated (design) |
| 3 — Theoretical | Forms require multiple inference steps; typologically plausible | ◇ | Speculative (design) |

ProtoAlante reconstructions are marked with an asterisk (*). Reconstructions inferred only from a single daughter are marked (*?). **Per §0, only Tier 1 carries any empirical weight, and even that weakly; Tiers 2–3 are design proposals, not findings.**

### 2.2 New correspondences proposed in this study

**C21 — ProtoAlante *w in onset position:**

| ProtoAlante | Angélico | Pavanam | Evidence |
|---|---|---|---|
| *w- (onset) | ʋ- | b- | *wan- → Ang. ʋan-, Pav. baor (§4, CS6) |

Angélico consistently shifts *w → ʋ (C19, established). Pavanam appears to *harden* an onset glide to a stop: *w → b. This is typologically attested (glide hardening under consonant lenition reversal, e.g., Proto-Germanic *w → /v/ in German). Evidence is limited to one cognate set; flagged as Tier 2.

**C22 — ProtoAlante *bʱ in coda (word-final root position):**

| ProtoAlante | Angélico | Pavanam | Evidence |
|---|---|---|---|
| *bʱ (onset) | bʱ | b | C7 (established) |
| *bʱ (coda) | bʱ | m | *tebʱ- → Ang. tebʱ-, Pav. tama (§4, CS5) |

In coda position, breathy-voiced stops lose stop articulation. The bilabial feature is retained in Pavanam as /m/. This is consistent with Pavanam's general tendency to lose breathy voice (C7–C9): coda *bʱ gives /m/ (nasal sonorant) rather than plain /b/ because coda position favors sonority. Conditioned sound law: **PA *bʱ → Pav. /m/ / _]root**.

**C23 — ProtoAlante initial *ŋ with following *r:**

| ProtoAlante | Angélico | Pavanam | Evidence |
|---|---|---|---|
| *ŋ-r- | ɹ- (metathesis; ŋ lost, r fronted) | ŋ-r- | *ŋardan → Ang. raddan, Pav. ŋarma (§4, CS4) |

In Angélico, when initial *ŋ is lost (C15) before a syllable containing *r, the /r/ moves to word-initial position by compensatory metathesis. Gemination of the following consonant optionally follows. This restructuring is triggered by the Angélico preference for a filled onset: losing ŋ- would leave a vowel-initial word, so /r/ shifts forward. Rule: **PA *ŋ-r → Ang. ɹ- / _ (compensatory metathesis)**.

## 3. Vowel Correspondences

ProtoAlante is reconstructed with three vowel qualities: *a / *e / *o, plus long counterparts *ā / *ē / *ō attested in the transitional vocabulary. Angélico preserves this three-vowel system. Pavanam has expanded to five vowels (a/e/i/o/u). The source of Pavanam /i/ and /u/ requires explanation.

**Working hypothesis:** Pavanam /i/ and /u/ derive from PA *e and *o respectively in consonantally conditioned environments, along the same pattern proposed for Classical Alantian (where /i/ = small/light and /u/ = large/heavy, under Theory 4 of the Philological Study).

| ProtoAlante | Angélico | Pavanam | Conditioning |
|---|---|---|---|
| *e | e | e (default) | before fricatives, sonorants |
| *e | e | i | before stops in closed syllables |
| *o | o | o (default) | before fricatives, sonorants |
| *o | o | u | before stops in closed syllables |
| *a | a | a | stable |

This remains a hypothesis; the conditioning environments require more data to confirm.

**Diphthong development in Pavanam:**

| ProtoAlante | Angélico | Pavanam |
|---|---|---|
| *ew (glide after *e) | eʋ | ei (glide vocalized: *w → i after *e) |
| *ow (glide after *o) | oʋ | au (glide vocalized and lowered: *w → u → au) |

Evidence: *ewes → Ang. eʋes / Pav. eihes (CS1); and *ewomen → Ang. eʋomen / Pav. auber (secondary evidence).

**CS3 — "fire"** ◆ (ProtoAlante → Pavanam only)

| Source | Form | IPA | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| ProtoAlante | *badar | /badar/ | [trans. Vocab.docx] |
| ProtoAlante | *badron | /badron/ | derivative form [trans.] |
| Angélico | pavkon | /paʋkon/ | [rom.] — probable innovation |
| Pavanam | bodrau | /bodrau/ | |

**Derivation PA *badar → Pav. bodrau:**  
*badar → *bodar (C4 *a→o; vowel backing in first syllable) → *bodra (syncope of medial *a, cluster *d+r formed, as already seen in PA *badron) → *bodrau (rhotic + diphthong ending, suffix innovation).

The intermediate form *bodron (parallel to attested PA *badron "conflagration") makes this derivation plausible: the -dr- cluster was already a PA internal variant. Pavanam extended it and innovated a new suffix *-au.

**Angélico pavkon:** No regular derivation from *badar has been found. The Angélico word is likely an independent innovation, possibly from a different PA root *baw- or a substrate word. The suffix -kon is the standard Angélico nominal suffix -on (< PA *-on, cf. *bʱefon, *gʱenon), so pavkon = /paʋ/ + /-kon/. The root /paʋ-/ remains unexplained from known PA sources.

## 5. The *ew- Root Word Family

The strongest structural evidence for a reconstructable ProtoAlante morphology is the *ew- root "water/liquid." In Angélico, this root appears in at least seven derived forms:

| Angélico form | IPA | Gloss | PA analysis |
|---|---|---|---|
| eʋes | /eʋes/ | water | *ew + *-es (mass noun suffix) |
| neʋem | /neʋem/ | waters, sea, rain | *n- + *ew + *-em (collective) |
| eʋoɹ | /eʋoɹ/ | year | *ew + *-or (cycle suffix?) |
| eʋomen | /eʋomen/ | river | *ew + *-omen (motion suffix) |
| eʋoxen- | /eʋoxen-/ | to wash | *ew + *-oxen- (verbal) |
| eʋen- | /eʋen-/ | to water, irrigate | *ew + *-en- (verbal applicative) |
| eʋana | /eʋana/ | wet (adj.) | *ew + *-ana (adj. suffix) |

The suffix system visible here is remarkably rich: *-es (mass noun), *-em (collective/plural), *-or (cyclic noun), *-omen (motion noun), *-oxen (verbal), *-en (verbal applicative), *-ana (adjectival). These suffixes appear elsewhere in the Angélico lexicon independently, suggesting they are genuine ProtoAlante morphological elements preserved in Angélico more completely than in Pavanam.

**Pavanam reflexes of the *ew- root:**

| Concept | Angélico | Pavanam | Note |
|---|---|---|---|
| water | eʋes (*ew-es) | eihes | CS1: *ew → ei before -es |
| river | eʋomen (*ew-omen) | auber | *ew → au before -o- |
| year | eʋoɹ (*ew-or) | ŋaire | different root; *ew-or not retained |
| sea | neʋem (*n-ew-em) | raita | different root; collective not retained |
| wet | eʋana (*ew-ana) | alba | different root in Pavanam |

Pavanam retained *ew- in two forms (eihes, auber) but replaced the extended forms with new vocabulary. This is consistent with Pavanam's general pattern of lexical innovation (§6).

## 6. The Lexical Replacement Problem

When the available cognate sets are tallied, a striking pattern emerges: the majority of basic vocabulary that is attested in both Angélico and Pavanam shows *no visible cognate relationship*.

| Semantic domain | Cognates found | Non-cognate pairs | Replacement rate |
|---|---|---|---|
| Primary elements | 2 (water, grass) | 3 (fire†, stone, earth) | ~60% |
| Body parts | 1 (tooth, theor.) | 12 (head, eye, ear, nose, tongue, neck, hand, foot, heart…) | ~92% |
| Animals | 0 | 5 (fish, dog, bird, animal, eagle) | 100% |
| Colors | 0 (all 4 replaced) | 4 | 100% |
| Numbers | 0 (all replaced) | 2 (one, two) | 100% |
| Weather | 1 (cloud, theor.) | 3 (sun, moon, rain) | ~75% |
| Basic adjectives | 1 (big, prob.) | 5 (small, hot, cold, good, new) | ~83% |
| Basic verbs | 1 (to come, prob.) | 6 (to eat, die, see, hear, sleep, walk) | ~86% |
| Pronouns | 0 | 2 (I, you) | 100% |

**Total across domains: ~5-6 probable/theoretical cognates out of ~50 testable pairs (~10-12% retention).**

A 10-12% cognate retention rate in basic vocabulary corresponds to an enormous time depth under normal Swadesh decay rates (roughly 86% per 1000 years for the 100-word list) — suggesting either >3,000 years of separation between Angélico and the Pavanam line, or systematic lexical replacement through one of the following mechanisms:

### 6.1 Time depth hypothesis

ProtoAlante → daughter languages may represent a deeper time division than the source documents imply. The transitional vocabulary (Vocab.docx) may not be the direct immediate ancestor of Angélico and Pavanam but an *older stratum* that predates both branches by many generations. Under this reading, ProtoAlante (Vocab.docx) is a reconstructed or archaic form, and both Angélico and Pavanam diverged from a later *post-Vocab.docx* ancestor for which we have no direct documentation.

### 6.2 Substrate replacement hypothesis

Both daughter languages may have absorbed vocabulary from contact languages (substrates), replacing the original H/P/C root-based lexicon in everyday vocabulary while retaining it in formal, poetic, or ritual speech. The ProtoAlante transitional forms (bʱebʱon "one," gʱenon "yellow," etc.) would then be the *archaic register* that was preserved in writing but replaced in colloquial use. The daughter languages' common vocabulary (Angélico's kokkos/beddes/sathon; Pavanam's ŋarma/arken/ranta) would be largely substrate-derived.

### 6.3 Independent parallel development

Some non-cognate pairs may be independently innovated synonyms that *replaced* shared PA roots in each branch separately. This is particularly likely for color terms (where PA *bʱefon, *gʱenon, *gʱobʱon, *kofon are all preserved only in transitional vocabulary and are replaced by completely different words in both daughters) and for numerals (where the bʱ-prefix reduplication system of PA was completely replaced by new counting terms in Angélico and Pavanam independently).

### 6.4 Design observation (meta-note)

In any constructed language family, some vocabulary non-correspondence across branches reflects the *separate creative act* of designing each language. This does not invalidate comparative analysis — it means that where cognates DO appear (the water root, the grass word, the tooth root, the fire word), they represent conscious design decisions to maintain family coherence, making them *more structurally important*, not less.

## 10. Summary: The Family's Comparative Profile

Applying the Comparative Method to the Alante family reveals three distinct phonological personalities and a vocabulary divided between shared inheritance and independent innovation:

**Angélico** is the most *conservative* branch: it retains the PA three-vowel system, the breathy-voiced series (as distinct phonemes), the bilabial fricative ɸ, and the PA suffix system most completely. Its major changes are peripheral: loss of ŋ, loss of l (with rhotic shift), hardening of h→ɰ, and glide shift w→ʋ. The *ew- root family is spectacularly productive in Angélico, suggesting that the PA morphological system was actively maintained and extended rather than eroded.

**Pavanam** is the most *innovative* branch: it lost the breathy-voice contrast entirely, developed a five-vowel system from the PA three-vowel base, created diphthongs from the PA *ew- glide sequence, hardened *w to /b/ in onset position, developed word-initial ŋ as a productive phoneme, and innovated the most dramatic lexical turnover. The percentage of non-cognate vocabulary in Pavanam (~88%) is consistent with a language that has undergone strong substrate influence or extended isolation.

**The lexical gap**: The transitional ProtoAlante vocabulary (colors, numerals, early pronouns) is not reflected in either daughter's basic vocabulary. These PA words were *replaced* — not by sound change so severe as to be unrecognizable, but by genuine lexical substitution. Whether this substitution happened before or after the split of the branches is unclear; the fact that both daughters replaced the SAME PA color terms and numerals with *different new words* suggests the replacements happened independently *after* the split, which in turn implies both daughter languages underwent strong substrate contact independently in their respective settlement areas.

**The s-invariance confirmation**: /s/ appears unchanged in every cognate set where it is present (CS1, CS9). No cognate set has been found where /s/ changed in any branch. This confirms the Philological Study's s-invariance principle empirically.
